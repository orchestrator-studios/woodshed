# Brief selection rules

The brief is one screen, read in thirty seconds, the day before a meeting. Everything else lives in the sheet — the brief links back to it, so cutting is safe. Three columns, each answering one question.

## Column 1 — "Who he is" (identity)

The person's professional identity in three short paragraphs: what they *are* (credentials, specialty, what they've built), where they sit (employer, key colleague/boss), and how insiders describe them (a quoted characterization beats your adjective). Top the column with the photo and a LinkedIn link. Career history and education go in a single muted line — supporting, not leading.

## Column 2 — "Where things stand" (the relationship)

This is the history of *the relationship*, not the person. Build a timeline of **state changes only** — the test for each candidate event: *would you describe the relationship differently after this email than before it?* Keep:

1. First appearance in the user's world.
2. Each status change (stranger → candidate → colleague; intro made; deal signed).
3. Direct-contact milestones — the first real conversation ("we've met" vs. "we've only emailed" changes how you walk in).
4. The most recent interaction, always — it's what the next conversation picks up from.

Drop scheduling replies, cc's, and passing mentions: they fill time between changes without being changes. Target ~6 lines; with more history, keep the first event, the last two, and the biggest status changes.

End with a **"Now" line**, different in kind: current standing, built from (a) the unresolved item — found by *comparing across time* (a start date that's been "coming weeks" for seven weeks is invisible in any single email), and (b) the agreed-upon future (planned scope, next steps).

## Column 3 — "Before you walk in" (the actionable part)

Gets the visual emphasis — it's the part that changes behavior in the room. Two sub-lists:

**Watch-outs** — admission test: *could you damage something or embarrass yourself by not knowing this?* If nothing clears that bar, the list is short or empty; never promote a minor item to fill space, because one inflated watch-out teaches the user to ignore all of them. Three kinds qualify, in stakes order, hard cap ~3:

1. **Tripwires** — combinations of true facts that create a hazard no single email states (hire is confidential + their current boss will be on the call). Phrase as a rule usable live ("if X is on the invite, then…"), not as trivia. Finding these — "what combinations of facts create a trap?" — is the highest-value inference in the whole brief.
2. **The user's own open loops** — questions they raised that never got answered, with who to chase before the meeting.
3. **The record's blind spots** — what the research can't see (preferred chat channel means email isn't the whole story). An honest brief flags its own limits.

**Easy openers** — test flips to lowest-effort rapport, highest information return: one item where *their expertise meets the user's work*, one item of *pending shared business* (signals you're read-in), one *personal/logistical softener*. Each must be raisable cold in the first two minutes. Verify personal details before suggesting them — one offhand mention ("I'll be on a tennis court") is thin; a firm-bio hobby is solid.

## Cross-cutting rules

- **Source chips**: every bullet in columns 2 and 3 ends with a small ↗ link to its source email (from the Email Mentions tab links). Claims resting on two sources get two chips. This is what lets the user trust a one-liner.
- **Tab disposition**: Contact & Bio feeds column 1; Email Mentions + Meetings feed columns 2–3; Context feeds the "Now" line and watch-outs. **News feeds the brief only when the meeting is external-facing** (pitch, negotiation, assessment); for a colleague-to-be it stays in the sheet.
- **Header**: name, compiled date, link to the sheet ("full backgrounder →"). Nothing else — detail belongs in the columns.
- **Fit one screen.** If a column overflows, cut the weakest item rather than shrinking type. The sheet link is the pressure valve.
